# "The Battle Won by Four Trumpets" — script v1 (2026-11-21)
Topic: Napoleon's invasion of Italy (1796), Battle of Arcole, 15-17 Nov 1796.
Target ~67 s at ~3.7 words/s (~245 words).

## Facts + sources
- Napoleon (born Aug 1769) was 27 and commanding the French Army of Italy in 1796.
- Arcole lies in the marshes of the Alpone/Adige east of Verona; the French had to advance along narrow dikes to the Arcole bridge. Fighting lasted 15-17 Nov 1796.
- 15 Nov: Napoleon seized a flag and led a charge on the dike. His aide Jean-Baptiste Muiron was killed beside him (the "shielded him with his body" detail is a later legend, so the script only says "shot dead beside him"). In the retreat Napoleon ended up in the marsh/ditch and was pulled out by his officers (accounts name Marmont and his brother Louis).
- 17 Nov: a small party of Guides with trumpeters went round the Austrian flank and sounded the charge. The Austrians took it for a large force and fell back. Accounts differ on the numbers (often "a few dozen Guides and four trumpeters"), and Augereau's column crossing downstream also helped. The hook's "four trumpets" is deliberate comic shorthand.
- Antoine-Jean Gros painted "Bonaparte at the Pont d'Arcole" (1796-97), showing him with the flag on the bridge. He never crossed the bridge during the charge.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Battle of Arcole"; Wikipedia "Jean-Baptiste Muiron"; worldhistory.org/article/2233/battle-of-arcole; napoleon.org (Gros painting notes); D. Chandler, The Campaigns of Napoleon (1966); M. Boycott-Brown, The Road to Rivoli (2001).

## Script
Napoleon once won a three-day battle with four trumpets and a few guys on horses.
It's November 1796, in the swamps of northern Italy, and Napoleon is a twenty-seven-year-old general with a hungry army and one tiny bridge in his way.
For two days, his men charge down a narrow dike toward the bridge at Arcole, and for two days the Austrians shove them right back.
So Napoleon grabs a flag and leads the charge himself.
The Austrians open fire, his own aide is shot dead beside him, and the retreat sweeps the general straight off the dike.
The man about to conquer Europe is up to his neck in swamp mud, and his officers have to drag him out.
On day three, he tries something else.
He sends a handful of riders and four trumpeters through the reeds, around behind the Austrian lines.
Then they blow the charge as loud as they can.
The exhausted Austrians hear cavalry behind them, panic, and pull back.
You see, in a swamp you can't see more than a few steps through the reeds, so your ears decide how big the enemy is.
Ever heard footsteps behind you on a dark street? Your brain doesn't count them. It just assumes the worst.
To the Austrians, four trumpets sounded like a whole regiment.
Luckily for Napoleon, a painter turned the battle into a legend.
Unluckily for the truth, the painting shows him charging across the bridge, flag in hand, and he never even made it onto the bridge.
